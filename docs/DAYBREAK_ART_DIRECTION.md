# DAYBREAK — Light reveals the work

Art-direction proposal · September 10, 2026

This is a concept and motion treatment. It does not change the application or the published site. It supersedes the rejected wheel/dial interpretation and the subsequent vertically scrolling surface treatment as the proposed interaction direction.

## 1. The experience in one sentence

The visitor stands before one immense dawn; scrolling advances the sunrise, and its changing light progressively reveals the portfolio within a stationary viewport.

The camera does not descend through a document. The sun is allowed to rise, atmosphere can drift laterally, and the work can appear, recede, and change. The page itself never travels vertically through the frame.

## 2. The decision: spend the whole experience inside one sunrise

Choose a single extended sunrise rather than a complete sunrise-to-sunset cycle for this direction.

DAYBREAK already has a strong identity. A full day would introduce several competing climaxes, require a separate visual vocabulary for noon and evening, and end with the light disappearing. One sunrise lets a small physical event carry a large amount of discovery. The final state feels like a beginning: the work has become clear, and the day remains ahead.

This is an authored expansion of a moment, not an astronomical simulation. The first frame already contains a visible edge of sun. We will not ask the visitor to endure several dark sections before the central idea becomes apparent.

## 3. The art: an atmosphere becoming a graphic composition

The central contrast is between soft air and very precise structure.

The sun begins as light partially obscured by a horizon. As it emerges, it becomes an almost impossibly clean, flat disc. Around it, fine contours gradually become legible. The work retains sharp typography, recognizable product imagery, and clear evidence. This lets atmospheric space and editorial graphic design coexist without reducing either to decoration.

Keep the established DAYBREAK material: oversized sans-serif typography, restrained serif italics, midnight and mineral blues, the coral disc, the real Gathr capture, VibeGrid artwork, Murmur's labeled interface study, and the existing line and ribbon studies. Recompose these into fixed frames; do not replace them with a new dashboard, illustrated landscape, or visual identity.

### The persistent world

- One sun, with continuous position and a consistent apparent size. Its visibility changes through occlusion; it never becomes a different decorative circle at each project.
- One horizon. It is so broad and shallow that it is felt as a curve before it is recognized as one. It occupies approximately the lower third of the opening desktop composition.
- A restrained family of horizontal and slightly curved contour lines. Their spacing and illumination carry depth. Avoid mountainous terrain or literal ocean reflections.
- A stable reading territory. The environment can evolve around it, but contrast remains intentional throughout.

A slight horizontal drift can imply that an enormous surface is turning beyond the frame. It is subordinate to the sunrise and is not a standalone rotation effect. The interface has no visible sphere, orbit diagram, compass, dial, or carousel.

### The distinctive gesture

The work appears to have been present in the world before the light reached it.

That does not mean hiding readable copy behind translucent fog. An incoming composition is suggested first by a faint edge or a single line; then its image and title resolve into a complete, high-contrast reading state. The impression of discovery comes from a short, controlled transition around a stable composition.

Use the direction of the incoming light to motivate the reveal. A narrow illuminated boundary crosses an image crop laterally; the image becomes fully visible behind it. The title resolves as a complete phrase. Supporting text follows once the dominant elements are settled. Avoid individually animated letters and unrelated staggered entrances.

The light boundary is a transition mask, not a permanent glowing stripe or spotlight cursor.

### Geometry and material

The earlier brief's reduction/abundance tension remains useful. The opening has one line and one emerging disc. Midway, the light reveals denser repeated geometry. The close returns to a disc, a few words, and open air.

Rhythm comes from repeated modules, changes in spacing, precise negative space, and cropped structures. Cultural references remain structural rather than ornamental. No decorative scripts, literal ethnic motifs, or radial mandala-like patterns.

Very fine grain can keep large atmospheric fields from feeling sterile. Keep it away from product images and readable type. Grain should disappear as an object of attention; it should not look like a retro film overlay.

### What makes these one scene

The sun never resets. The horizon does not cut away. The light evolves continuously. A limited set of alignment lines persists even when the project layout changes. The incoming and outgoing compositions briefly share those alignments, so the visitor senses continuity instead of six unrelated full-screen panels.

Do not invent a new visual trick for every transition. The common revealing-light treatment is the grammar. The project's content, scale, and density give each exposure its character.

### The Gathr moment

This remains the strongest poster composition, preserving its role in the earlier design. When the disc clears the horizon, the capture should already be readable. Do not simultaneously enlarge the phone, rotate the headline, move the clouds rapidly, and change the type scale. Give the sun the movement; give the work the attention.

### The final frame

It should feel open rather than completed or shut down. Contact remains available and stationary. No automatic restart, sunset fade-to-black, bounce into the beginning, or endless loop.

## 5. Animation is the structure

The mouse wheel and touch gesture control progress through an authored timeline. The visible stage remains fixed. Scrolling backward reverses the same timeline. Stopping input stops meaningful progression after a short settling response.

There are three kinds of motion, with clear priority:

1. **Environmental motion:** the continuous sunrise, changing sky illumination, and restrained lateral atmospheric drift.
2. **Disclosure:** the brief, light-motivated handover from one complete content composition to another.
3. **Direct interaction:** a visitor deliberately manipulates a study, opens project evidence, or selects another destination.

The environmental layer should not compete with the disclosure layer. The content should not continue wandering while someone is reading or manipulating it.

### Transition choreography

For a representative transition between projects:

- First portion: the outgoing project remains legible while a subtle atmospheric change indicates continuation.
- Middle portion: supporting copy recedes; the outgoing image and title relinquish the composition. An illuminated edge introduces the next image. At most one project carries full readable emphasis.
- Final portion: the incoming image and title settle; supporting copy and actions become fully available.
- Reading interval: the complete composition holds. The sun may advance very slightly with further input, but the layout stays quiet.

Transitions should account for a minority of the total journey. Reading intervals should occupy roughly two-thirds or more. These are scroll-distance allocations: they do not force the user to wait for a timer.

The reveal is reversible and driven by timeline position. Do not use a set of one-way “play once when visible” triggers or a collection of independently timed CSS entrances.

### Input behavior

- No autoplay tour on arrival.
- No forced minimum viewing time or fixed three-second transition that traps the user.
- Fast input may skip ahead; the interface must resolve cleanly to the requested state.
- A short smoothing response can soften coarse mouse-wheel input. It must settle quickly and must not make trackpad input feel disconnected.
- Navigation offers direct destinations. Selecting Work or Contact should not replay every intervening scene.
- Preserve the timeline position when returning from project evidence.
- Interactive controls own their usual keys and gestures. A range slider, focused button, or active sculpture drag must not accidentally advance the story.

## 6. Legibility is part of the composition

The current scenes cannot simply be made sticky at their existing document heights. Their content must be composed for the actual available frame.

For a primary project state, budget for one display heading, one short explanation, one concrete proof statement, one media object, and an obvious route into further evidence. Remove redundant on-stage labels before reducing body-text size.

Maintain a stable, calm area behind body copy. If the light makes a text color change necessary, perform the change between readable states; do not gradually interpolate body text through low-contrast colors.

Project captures keep their original colors, proportions, and labeling. The art can frame or crop their outer container, but it must not blur their evidence or masquerade generated imagery as a working product.

Long-form project evidence remains a deliberate reading experience, distinct from scrubbing the main sunrise. The existing case-study routes can supply this without rebuilding them. This is a content decision to preserve usability, not permission to send every homepage interaction away from the main composition. The full project index, experiments, about summary, and contact remain accessible within the main experience.

## 7. Mobile is another composition of the same sunrise

Preserve the same sun, horizon, palette progression, and project order. Do not shrink the desktop frame or add a permanent miniature dial.

Portrait layouts use fewer simultaneous elements and one dominant alignment. A project may need a second stable disclosure state for its supporting evidence. Add a state rather than squeeze a desktop heading, full phone capture, and multiple paragraphs into an unreadable stack.

Author the first layout studies at desktop 1440 × 900 and portrait 390 × 844, then test short screens and enlarged text. Browser chrome, safe areas, and navigation reduce usable height; the composition must use that remaining area rather than assume a nominal device size.

Essential content must not be clipped merely to maintain a cinematic frame. A calm reading view is the fallback for enlarged text or constrained viewports where the fixed composition cannot remain usable.

## 8. Reduced motion and access

Reduced motion retains the authored compositions and project sequence. Replace spatial travel and continuous sunrise motion with stable states and immediate state changes, or a minimal dissolve if the user's preference allows it. Provide clearly named previous/next or direct chapter controls.

Only the active content state participates in keyboard focus and the accessibility tree. Keep navigation consistently available. Moving into an explicit reading view pauses the main timeline, and returning restores its previous position.

A readable static document remains available before enhancement and if scripting or graphics fail. This is a fallback, not the primary visual experience the user requested.

## 9. Rendering decisions follow the art

The proposed scene does not need a visible Three.js sphere. Its dimensional character comes mainly from composition, controlled occlusion, and continuous environmental state.

- **HTML:** readable type, actual project information, links, controls, and semantic structure.
- **SVG:** the authored sun/horizon relationships, revealing masks, precise contour fields, and existing vector studies.
- **CSS:** layout, atmospheric color fields, layer compositing, and limited transforms.
- **Three.js:** retain the existing interactive ribbon where spatial manipulation is the actual content. Add more only if a specific approved visual effect demonstrably requires it.

A single timeline supplies solar position, illumination, active composition, and transition progress. Fixed framing is the primary layout architecture. It cannot be achieved by adding another subtle transform to the current vertically scrolling sections.

A native scroll range can provide input behind a pinned stage. The visitor's visible result is the animation; the stage never translates with the document. This avoids treating raw wheel-event interception as the default architecture. The exact input method should be validated in the later motion study, including mouse, trackpad, touch, keyboard, and navigation.

## 10. Before implementation

The next design deliverable should be six actual composition stills using the existing typography and project media, plus one transition breakdown between two adjacent states. The atmosphere board accompanying this document is a study of continuity and light, not final UI artwork.

Review those stills for composition, emotional progression, project clarity, and continuity. Then make one isolated motion study of a single transition, outside the production homepage. It should demonstrate that scroll advances light and disclosure while the frame remains stationary. Expand only after that mechanism conveys the intended experience.

Do not replace the whole homepage to test an interpretation. Preserve the current publication throughout this design stage.

The standard for the concept is specific: at any paused point, the viewport should look intentionally composed; in motion, the next frame should feel like the same world at a later moment. The work should be easier to understand as the light arrives.

## Atmosphere study — critique and limits

An original six-frame raster study was generated with the built-in imagegen tool for concept review only. It is not referenced by the application and is not a deployment asset.

The study supports the blue-to-copper-to-silver progression and the contrast between the clean disc and fine line work. It is not yet the final art direction: flatten the horizon further so it reads less like a hill, reduce the apparent grain, and enforce an exactly consistent sun radius and horizon alignment. The final compositions need the actual portfolio typography and evidence; an attractive empty sky alone does not establish the portfolio's originality.
