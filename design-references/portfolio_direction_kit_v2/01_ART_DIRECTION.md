# 01 — Portfolio Art Direction

## 1. Creative thesis

The portfolio should explore **daybreak indirectly**.

Do not directly show:
- a visible sun
- a literal sunrise scene
- a horizon
- a scenic room
- landscape silhouettes
- cliché morning icons

Instead, the site should show **what dawn does to a surface**.

Morning is expressed through:
- light entering from outside the frame
- soft window-shadow geometry
- gradual tonal warming
- increasing clarity
- a subtle but richer dawn palette
- restrained motion
- the feeling that the page is slowly waking up

The central concept is:

> **a minimalist surface gradually illuminated by morning.**

---

## 2. Updated color thesis

Earlier, the site was imagined as mostly neutral with restrained warmth.

That still holds, but this version should carry **more of the colors of dawn**.

Not in a loud gradient-heavy way.  
Not as an obvious “sunrise graphic.”  
But through a carefully controlled spread of sunrise-adjacent hues across light, shadow, atmosphere, and certain compositional accents.

Suggested emotional color notes:
- soft peach
- pale apricot
- muted coral
- blush-tinted cream
- dusty gold
- straw light
- warm ivory
- faint rose
- misted lavender-grey
- blue-grey pre-dawn coolness

The page should move from:
- cooler / dimmer / quieter
toward:
- warmer / more luminous / more chromatically alive

The palette should still feel elegant and minimal.

---

## 3. The surface is the stage

The site may still be mostly plain.

Keep the visual power of a quiet background.

The background can imply:
- paper
- plaster
- stone
- matte card
- linen
- washi-like texture
- frosted translucency

But it should never become a realistic scenic background.

The surface exists so that:
- light becomes visible
- color can bloom softly across it
- shadows can read
- type can sit clearly
- motion has meaning

The page should feel **material**, not decorated.

---

## 4. Minimalism × Japanese-influenced sensibility

The Japanese influence should remain structural, not thematic.

### 4.1 Ma — meaningful emptiness
Use:
- generous whitespace
- pauses
- sparse sectioning
- breathing room around important content

### 4.2 Restraint
Use:
- few but carefully chosen colors
- one coherent shadow language
- a small set of motion ideas
- few decorative gestures

### 4.3 Material sensitivity
Let surfaces feel softly physical.

### 4.4 Ephemerality
The site should feel aware of passing light and color.

### 4.5 Precision
Minimalism must be executed exactly:
- spacing
- type
- rhythm
- shadow position
- timing
- color balance

The Japanese influence should come through as:

> emptiness, precision, atmosphere, impermanence, and quiet asymmetry.

Never through clichés.

---

## 5. Emotional character

The portfolio should feel:

- calm
- spacious
- tactile
- refined
- contemplative
- precise
- softly radiant
- intimate but not cozy
- warm but not sugary
- poetic but still disciplined
- minimal but not sterile

The mood should be closer to:

> early morning light slowly filling a blank surface

than to a vivid scenic sunrise.

---

## 6. Typography as atmosphere

Typography remains one of the primary expressive tools.

The type should feel:
- elegant
- editorial
- controlled
- highly legible
- quiet but confident

Possible typographic behavior:
- slightly quieter contrast at the top
- stronger clarity as the page progresses
- subtle relationship to light and warm color shifts
- a sense that the text is becoming more present as morning arrives

Do not:
- distort text
- overanimate text
- let color shifts reduce contrast or legibility

---

## 7. Daybreak progression

The page may evolve as the user scrolls.

### Hero / first impression
- cooler undertones
- more white space
- long, soft shadows
- very quiet warmth

### Early sections
- light begins to warm
- faint peach/gold notes emerge
- content becomes more defined

### Mid-page
- dawn is more present
- gentle apricot/coral/blush presence can appear in light, shadow, or accents
- atmosphere feels more open

### Later sections
- calm morning clarity
- warmer neutral field
- a mature daylight feeling with lingering dawn color memory

This should be a continuum, not discrete “chapters.”

---

## 8. Light and shadow language

Use **soft geometric shadow forms** to imply off-screen light.

Possible cues:
- window-frame shadows
- diagonal light planes
- rectangular light patches
- long falloff
- faint warm glows
- slowly shifting color-tinted illumination

Important update:

The light itself can now carry more nuanced dawn color.

For example:
- a cool neutral base shadow field
- a warm apricot light wash
- a pale gold edge glow
- a faint rose-lavender transitional zone

Still keep it restrained.

Avoid:
- theatrical beams
- over-saturated gradients
- obvious color bands
- poster-like sunset effects

---

## 9. Globe-like scroll concept

The spatial metaphor remains:

> The viewport sees only a tiny visible patch of an enormous curved surface.

As the user scrolls:
- content still behaves like normal vertical content
- the center stays nearly flat
- content may recede or shift slightly near the edges
- the page may feel like it is gently revolving

This should be secondary to the dawn atmosphere.

If the spatial effect competes with the color/light effect, tone it down.

---

## 10. Anti-goals

Reject anything that feels:
- loud
- cinematic
- neon
- “creative-coder showreel”
- over-gradient-heavy
- like a visible sun is hiding somewhere
- like a Japanese-themed moodboard
- like an animation demo

Avoid:
- giant glowing orb
- obvious orange-pink hero gradient
- over-saturated peach/coral blocks
- visible 3D sphere
- heavy parallax
- aggressive WebGL effects
- constant floating movement

---

## 11. Success criteria

The design is working when a visitor thinks:

- “This feels like dawn.”
- “The color is beautiful but restrained.”
- “The page feels alive without being busy.”
- “The atmosphere seems to wake up as I move through it.”
- “The animation supports the feeling instead of showing off.”
- “The site feels authored and calm.”

It is failing when they think:

- “This is a sunrise-themed landing page.”
- “The gradients are pretty, but distracting.”
- “The animation is the main point.”
- “The site is trying too hard to be aesthetic.”
- “The content got lost.”
